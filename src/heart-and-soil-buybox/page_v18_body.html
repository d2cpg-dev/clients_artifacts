What we measured

Take rate here is the share of new-customer orders that contain a subscription. A cart holding a subscription and a one-off item counts once. Renewals are excluded from both sides, and the TikTok channel is excluded because the buy box never appears there, as is The Lobby, which is creator seeding rather than purchasing. Over {{denom_window}} this denominator is {{ck_denom}} orders. Your own reporting shows {{ck_denom_exp}} for the same window, and the difference is exactly the {{ck_seed}} seeding orders we now leave out.

What changed in the buy box

Nothing was removed. The buy box swapped which group of plans it puts in front of people. The group that used to take almost every sign-up went from {{fam_old_pre}} subscriptions before the change to {{fam_old_post}} since. The group that replaced it went from {{fam_new_pre}} to {{fam_new_post}}. Both groups existed before the change.

What the new group does differently is tie how much you buy to how often it arrives. At {{gone_cad}} days a single bottle used to be {{one90_old}}% of that cycle's sign-ups and is now {{one90_new}}%, while three bottles went from {{three90_old}}% to {{three90_new}}%. At {{alive_cad}} days one bottle went from {{one60_old}}% to {{one60_new}}% and two bottles from {{two60_old}}% to {{two60_new}}%. At {{m30_cad}} days, where one bottle is the natural quantity anyway, almost nothing moved: {{one30_old}}% to {{one30_new}}%.

So the long cycles have become bulk purchases. Somebody who wants one bottle at a time is now steered to the monthly plan. The combinations themselves are all still buyable, quietly: {{live30}} people started a single bottle monthly since the sale, {{live60}} did it at {{alive_cad}} days and {{live90}} at {{gone_cad}} days, the most recent on {{live90_last}}.

If you count dollars instead of orders

The take rate above counts decisions. A cart holding a subscription and a one-off counts once, because the customer either chose a subscription on that visit or did not. A fair question is what the same period looks like weighted by money rather than by orders, so we ran it.

Measured that way, subscription revenue is {{rw_pre}}% of what new customers spent before the change and {{rw_post}}% since, a fall of {{rw_drop}} points against the {{drop_r}} points the order count shows. The gap between those two numbers is not a disagreement. It is the whole story in one line: fewer customers subscribe, and the ones who still do commit more money each time, because subscription spend per subscribing order went from ${{rw_sub_ord_pre}} to ${{rw_sub_ord_post}}, {{rw_sub_ord_pct}}. The larger basket cancels most of the smaller headcount.

That is the finding the money view adds. Subscription revenue per day moved {{rw_subday_pct}}, which is standing still. Everything else new customers bought moved {{rw_oneday_pct}}. Total new-customer revenue is up {{rev_pct}} a day, and all of that increase comes from the non-subscription side, from customers who would previously have subscribed and now buy once instead, at a larger basket.

So the two ways of counting point the same way. The order count says fewer people are choosing a subscription. The money count says the subscription business itself is no bigger than it was. The forward-looking figure above agrees with both: committed spend per subscription per month is flat, because the larger order now arrives less often.

Method and limits

We hold any read on a change that overlaps a promotion until there are at least {{clean_rule}} clean days after the promotion ends. The earlier read on this question was taken before those days existed, which is why this one reverses it.

How we measured it+
  • Take rate counts orders rather than line items, so a cart holding both a subscription and a one-off item counts once. Renewals are removed from both sides. Two sales channels are excluded throughout: TikTok, where the buy box never appears, and The Lobby, which is creator seeding. Shopify records no gross sales, no net sales and one item on every Lobby order, and none has ever carried a subscription, so they would only dilute both the take rate and average order value.
  • Windows: baseline {{pre_start}} to {{pre_end}} excluding {{promo_long}}, launch and Labor Day sale {{sale_long}}, since the sale {{post_long}}.
  • {{promo_long}} is excluded for two independent reasons: a sitewide cart promotion, and a Skio migration of {{migration}} records landing in the same days.
  • Migration records are excluded from the plan analysis. The pre-change base is {{plan_base}} subscriptions rather than the {{plan_base_bloated}} that would appear if they were left in.
  • Plan mix covers the {{n_products}} single products where the tiered buy box lives. That is {{cov_pre}}% of subscriptions created before the change and {{cov_post}}% since, a spread of {{cov_spread}} points, so it cannot bias a comparison.
  • Subscription plans are read as they stand today rather than as first chosen. Only {{drift_pre}}% of pre-change subscriptions have ever had a line edited, against {{drift_post}}% since the change.
  • Every subscription dollar figure is a list price. The Skio export carries priceWithoutDiscount and no charged price on the subscription line, the order line or the change log.
  • Sessions and conversion are store-wide rather than new-customer only, so they set context and do not decompose the take rate.
  • Confidence interval is a day-level cluster bootstrap over {{boot_n}} resamples, cross-checked against a quasi-binomial interval with a dispersion correction of {{phi}}. Both are reported in the figures table.
  • Shopify data is a set of five exports pulled {{pulled_long}} covering {{range_long}}. The Skio pull covers the same window. {{tail_note}}
  • The new-customer denominator over {{denom_window}} is {{ck_denom}} orders against the {{ck_denom_exp}} in your earlier reporting, a difference of exactly the {{ck_seed}} seeding orders now excluded. Across the whole range that channel accounts for {{ck_seed_total}} orders. Two wider counts over {{ck_window}} do not: this pull gives {{ck_orders}} orders and {{ck_signups}} tagged sign-ups against the {{ck_orders_exp}} and {{ck_signups_exp}} reported earlier. The September export separates the subscription app into its own sales channel, so the totals are not drawn on the same base. Every new-customer figure in this report reproduces the earlier pull day for day.
