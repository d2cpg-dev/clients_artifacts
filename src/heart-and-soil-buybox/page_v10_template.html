The buy box earns more per subscriber and signs up fewer. One missing option explains both.

First, a correction

Three weeks ago we told you take rate was flat. That was wrong, and the mistake was ours: we called it on five days of orders, three of which were the Labor Day sale, and the sale hid the decline. Your read was right and ours wasn't — please treat that earlier conclusion as withdrawn.

We've changed how we work as a result. No trend call until we have a clean window after a promotion ends.

With the sale behind us, the share of first-time orders containing a subscription has fallen about 6 points, from {{bef}}% before the change to {{aft}}% across Sep 8–16. The customers who do subscribe are worth {{dval}} more at full price, ${{valb}} to ${{vala}} per subscription, so every 100 first-time orders now carries {{dord}} more subscription value than before.

One fact explains both halves. The old buy box let a customer take a single bottle every 60 days or longer, and 28% of new subscribers chose exactly that. The new box's smallest option is one bottle a month. That plan didn't lose share — it stopped existing.

This is not an argument for reverting the buy box. Value per first-time order is up, not down, and the gap looks like one missing tier rather than a broken design.

Why — The Option That Disappeared

Old buy box
  • 1 bottle, every 30 days
  • 1 bottle, every 60 days
  • 1 bottle, every 90 days
  • More than one bottle only by setting the quantity by hand
New buy box
  • 1 bottle, every 30 days
  • 2 bottles, every 60 days
  • 3 bottles, every 90 days
  • No way to take one bottle less often than monthly

28% of new subscriptions used to be one bottle every 60 days or longer. That is now a fraction of it, and most of what remains is customers who changed their plan after signing up, since we read plans as they stand today rather than as first chosen.

A customer who wanted one bottle a quarter must now take three bottles a quarter, or one a month, or nothing. Fewer sign-ups and bigger baskets are the same fact seen from two sides.

What We Can't Separate

Layout or price

The entry tier got a 20% price rise on the same day as the new layout. Nothing in this data pulls them apart. A holdout group or a discount-only change would.

Temporary or permanent

Sep 8 to 16 sits immediately after a sale. Nine days can't tell a post-sale dip from a lasting effect. Oct 1 can.

Two gaps worth naming. We can't see anyone who bounced, because we have order data rather than session data. And we can't see what happened to customers already on the old low-commitment plans, because we don't have migration data.

Checks We Ran+
Falling traffic doesn't explain it

In the weeks before the change, busier days ran lower take rates, not higher. Traffic is down now, which should have pushed the rate up. Correcting for it makes the decline larger.

Two separate systems agree

Skio and Shopify don't feed each other. Subscriptions per first-time order fell 15% in Skio; the Shopify take rate fell 10% in relative terms. Same direction, independently.

Channel mix explains part of it

Some first-time orders moved to channels where the buy box never appears. On orders that actually saw the new box the drop is 3.6 points rather than 5.9.

The estimate is stable

Twelve model specifications all point the same way. Best estimate 6.1 points, 95% range 4.1 to 8.0, with standard errors corrected for orders within a day not being independent.

Method+
  • Take rate counts orders, not line items. A cart holding a subscription and a one-time item counts once. First-time customers are counted from the order line; returning customers from the subscription app's first-order tag. Renewals are removed from both sides.
  • Windows: before is Aug 6 to Sep 2, the launch and Labor Day sale is Sep 3 to 7, and after the sale is Sep 8 to 16.
  • Subscription plan is read as it stands today, not as chosen at signup, so a customer who has since edited their cadence shows in their current tier. Before-window totals run about 1% below the previous report for this reason.
  • Sep 18 returned no orders and Sep 17 is incomplete, so both are dropped. Including Sep 17 makes the decline look larger, not smaller.
  • Plan and value figures cover the 21 single products, which is where the three-tier box lives. Stacks were flat throughout, about $133 per order before and after.
  • Sessions, conversion and order value come from the weekly store report and run only to Sep 13, which is why that line stops earlier than the others.
