Subscription Buy-Box Review

Heart & Soil · all products · launched Thu Sep 3, 2026

The new buy box changed what subscribers buy. Whether it changed how many subscribe is not yet visible underneath the Labor Day promotion.

Store-wide subscription opt-in ran 55.2% in launch week against 52.0% over the previous four weeks. Labor Day alone reached 62.4%; the other six days ran 52.6%, level with the four-week average and 1.6 points under the 54.2% of Aug 12 to Sep 2, after an early-August promotion had passed. Of subscriptions started in launch week, 50% chose monthly, 15% bi-monthly, 32% quarterly, with quarterly nearly doubling from 17%. On single products, revenue per order rose 30%, driven by units per new subscription climbing from 1.15 to 1.92 rather than by price. List price per unit did not move.

How Many Subscribed

Orders that started a subscription as a share of orders, Aug 6 to Sep 9. The left-hand buttons cut the same orders four ways, by customer type and channel set; the right-hand pair switches between daily points and calendar weeks, Monday to Sunday, the grain the weekly reporting uses.

Returning customers moved more than new ones

New customers opt in far more often than returning ones: 57.1% against 45.6% over the four weeks before the launch, so the store-wide rate follows the customer mix as much as the buy box. In launch week returning customers went to 50.7%, +5.1 pt, and 47.4% without Labor Day; new customers went to 60.2%, +3.1 pt, and 57.7% without Labor Day. The buy box is a decision a customer meets at the product page, so the returning segment is where an effect would appear first. Still inside a promotion week, so read it as a direction.

Which Cadence They Chose

Every new subscription across the catalogue, grouped by the delivery cycle it started on. Shares sum to 100%.

The same cadence label meant a different product before Sep 3

The old buy box let a subscriber choose how often one unit shipped: every 30, 60 or 90 days. The new tiers ship one, two or three units per delivery. A quarterly subscription before the launch was one bottle every three months; after, it is three bottles every three months. Across single products, 1,322 of the 1,772 pre-launch quarterly subscriptions carried a single bottle (74.6%); after launch, 1,195 of 1,288 carry three or more (92.8%). Bi-monthly subscriptions moved the same way, 1,629 of 1,858 one bottle before (87.7%) to 382 of 612 two (62.4%). Units per new subscription rose from 1.15 to 1.92. The chart above compares billing cadence, which is the same axis in both periods. The product volume behind each cadence is not, and the value section below is where that difference shows up.

What It Did To Value

Single products only, since that is where the three-tier box lives. Three measures, each on its own scale.

What We Are Watching

Two boxes went live, with two different offers

Single products received the three supply tiers at 10 / 20 / 30% off, sustained on every order, with Buy Once reduced to a text link. Stack products received a single monthly option at 30% off the first order and 10% on renewals, with Buy Once kept as a full button. Both groups gained subscribers. Singles went from 26.5% to 37.6% of product orders, +11.1 pt, while their monthly discount fell from 25% to 10%. Stacks went from 36.3% to 62.0%, +25.7 pt, with a first-order discount that rose from 25% to 30% and roughly twice the traffic lift. The gap between the two tracks the discount depth on the first order, which is what a promotion week measures. The singles figure is the one carrying a price increase on its most popular tier and still rising.

Where the store-wide rate goes next

The launch-week rate is a promotion reading. The first clean measurement is the two weeks from Sep 14 to Sep 27, with no holiday and no sale traffic. If opt-in holds between 52 and 54% there, the two pre-launch readings, the box did not move it. If it sits above that band, it did. Either answer is useful and neither is available yet.

Open Items

  • Cost of goods per unit and shipping cost per shipment. With the discount table confirmed and units per subscription known, these two inputs turn the value picture into margin.
  • First clean read of the store-wide rate: Sep 14 to Sep 27, no holiday and no sale traffic in the window.
  • 348 orders on the subscription app's two channels carry neither a renewal tag nor a renewal order number; 100 of them carry a first-time protein-insert tag, which points to subscriptions added through the customer portal. If every one were a sign-up, the store-wide rate would read 52.5% before and 55.0% after. The returning-customer tag gap cannot be sized from these exports.
  • Since Sep 3, 59 in 100 new-customer subscription orders also carry a one-time line, against 5 in 100 before. One export of one-time line items by product for Sep 3 to 9 names what is riding on those orders.
  • Stack subscribers joined at 30% off and renew at 10%. The first renewals on those subscriptions fall in early October and will show whether the 20-point step-down holds them.
  • First quarterly renewals on single products land around Dec 3. That is the earliest date the retention question on 90-day cycles has an answer.

Method

  • Store-wide opt-in is orders that started a subscription divided by checkout orders. A new customer's order counts as a sign-up when it contains a subscription line, the attribute Shopify writes at checkout from the selling plan; a returning customer's order counts when it carries Skio's first-order tag, the only field that separates a first subscription order from a renewal. Both sides exclude renewals, identified by the Skio and LUNA recurring tags; orders on the subscription app's two channels, Skio Subscriptions and Heart & Soil Subscriptions, none of which came from a new customer or carried a first-order tag while 81.8% carried a renewal order number; and zero-revenue service channels: Gorgias, Corso, The Lobby, Trybe UGC, Superfiliate, DigitalGenius, Shopify Mobile, Draft Orders, GOAFFPRO. Earlier versions read 48.9% and 52.1%, then 48.3% and 51.8%, then 51.9% and 54.6%: the first removed service channels from the denominator only, the second kept untagged app-channel renewals in, the third counted new customers by tag and so missed 38 sign-ups before the launch and 41 in launch week.
  • For new customers the order line and Skio's tag disagree on 38 orders before the launch (0.6%) and 41 in launch week (2.2%), 32 of them on Sep 8, when tagging lagged the holiday; the line is used because it is written at checkout and cannot lag. By tag alone the new-customer rate excluding TikTok Shop would read 57.3% and 57.9% instead of 57.6% and 59.2%. The returning-customer gap cannot be sized, since for that segment the line attribute also marks renewals. Carts holding both a subscription line and a one-time line rose from 12 a day among new customers to 153 a day from Sep 3, so any report that adds the subscription and one-time rows instead of reading the distinct order total would show new-customer opt-in falling from 54.1% to 43.1%.
  • The opt-in chart offers four cuts of the same orders, by customer type and channel set, and two grains. Weekly points pool calendar weeks, Monday to Sunday, to match the weekly reporting; the window opens on a Thursday and closes on a Wednesday, so the last week holds 3 days, drawn hollow, and the week of Aug 31–Sep 6 holds 3 days before the launch and 4 after. All series come from the same two exports of orders by day, customer type and sales channel; only the filter changes.
  • The pre-launch window is not flat. Aug 7 to 11 carried a Boost Cart Reward promotion, 296 to 635 tagged orders a day against a median of 131 afterwards, and read 46.2% on this page's definition; Aug 12 to Sep 2 read 54.0%. Daily rates vary with a standard deviation of 2.4 points in that clean stretch and 4.6 across the six non-holiday launch days, so a six-day window cannot resolve a difference much under 4 points.
  • TikTok Shop orders, 845 in the window and none of them a sign-up, stay in this page's denominator. Removing them on both sides reads 53.6% before and 56.1% after and changes no conclusion.
  • Product take rate is Skio subscriptions created for the product divided by Shopify checkout orders containing it on Online Store and Shop, the two channels where the buy box renders. TikTok Shop and Meta shops carry no buy box and are excluded from this measure; including them shifts the singles figure by 0.2 pt.
  • An order with two products counts once toward each product's denominator. This is correct for product-scoped rates and inflates the singles group total relative to stacks, since singles dominate mixed carts. Correcting for it moves the singles take rate up about 2 pt.
  • Discounts are taken from the live storefront on Sep 11. Single products: 10% on 30-day supply, 20% on 60-day, 30% on 90-day, applied to every order. Stacks: 30% on the first order, 10% on renewals. Before Sep 3 all subscriptions ran a flat 25%.
  • Cadence is the delivery cycle recorded at opt-in. The 9,970 subscriptions written by the LUNA-to-Skio migration on Aug 6 and 7 are excluded; they carry a migration flag and were never sign-ups.
  • Shipping Protection is an add-on, not a product. It collapsed from about 110 to 12 per day on launch day through a configuration change and is excluded from every count.
  • Skio timestamps in UTC while Shopify uses store time. Daily points can shift by a few hours between sources; period totals are unaffected. Labor Day's evening orders appear on Sep 8 in Skio and Sep 7 in Shopify.
  • New versus returning is Shopify's classification at order time, taken from the store-level export, so each order counts once.
  • After-period figures cover 7 days including a holiday. Volume is compared per day; rates and shares are proportions.
