What We Measured

Take rate here is the share of new-customer orders that contain a subscription. A cart holding a subscription and a one-off item counts once. Renewals are excluded from both sides, and the TikTok channel is excluded because the buy box never appears there. Over Aug 6 to Sep 2 this denominator is 11,823 orders, which reproduces the figure behind your earlier reporting exactly.

The baseline is the 40 clean days from Jul 20 to Sep 2. We cut Aug 7 to 11 out of it for two independent reasons: a sitewide cart promotion ran across those days, and a Skio data migration of 55,410 records landed in the same window.

Two details in that chart carry most of the weight. The sale peak is real and it is large: Labor Day itself ran 69.17% on 1.8 times a normal day's orders, which is why a window containing it tells you very little. And the fall was already there on day one. Sep 3, the first day the new buy box was live and before the sale had built, read 51.41%.

How Sure We Are

The daily numbers barely overlap. The weakest of the 40 days before the change was 54.74%. 8 of the 9 days since the sale came in below that. The single exception reached 57.27%. Comparing every post-change day against every pre-change day, the post day is lower in 354 of 360 pairings, which on a rank test that assumes nothing about the shape of the data gives a probability of about 7 in 1,000,000.

Every way we cut it lands between 7.0 and 8.6 points, and when we ran the same method on 18 earlier dates where nothing had happened, the largest false fall it produced was 1.8 points against the 7.8 we are reporting.

How we cut it+

The order-tag measure gives 8.5 points. Standardising for day of week gives 7.8 points. Comparing only against pre-change days at similar traffic gives 8.6 points, across 8 days averaging 29,840 sessions against 27,806 now. The online store on its own gives 7.0 points.

Ruled outTraffic does not explain it

Across the 40 clean days before the change, daily take rate and daily sessions are effectively uncorrelated, at a correlation of -0.03. Restricting the comparison to pre-change days at similar traffic widens the fall rather than narrowing it.

Ruled outIt is not a tracking gap

99.5% of subscriptions created since the sale trace to a Shopify order the same day. Shopify reports more subscribing orders than Skio holds subscriptions in every window we checked, so there is no pool of sign-ups going unrecorded.

CheckedReading plans as they stand today is safe

Only 4.4% of pre-change subscriptions have ever had a line edited, and 0.8% within their first 9 days. That is far too little to manufacture the basket shift below.

Still openNo verdict on permanence yet

We cannot call permanence on 9 days, and we have no year-ago data for this window. A seasonal effect would have to arrive as a step inside one week and then hold. Seasonality does not behave that way. We still cannot close it with data we do not have.

What Changed In The Buy Box

Nothing was removed. The buy box swapped which group of plans it puts in front of people. The group that used to take almost every sign-up went from 12,406 subscriptions before the change to 165 since. The group that replaced it went from 542 to 2,605. Both groups existed before the change.

What the new group does differently is tie how much you buy to how often it arrives. At 90 days a single bottle used to be 46.9% of that cycle's sign-ups and is now 2.4%, while three bottles went from 18.6% to 75.2%. At 60 days one bottle went from 61.1% to 31.1% and two bottles from 25.0% to 59.7%. At 30 days, where one bottle is the natural quantity anyway, almost nothing moved: 72.1% to 72.9%.

So the long cycles have become bulk purchases. Somebody who wants one bottle at a time is now steered to the monthly plan. The combinations themselves are all still buyable, quietly: 1,020 people started a single bottle monthly since the sale, 151 did it at 60 days and 23 at 90 days, the most recent on Sep 16.

What It Is Worth

Each subscription now commits more product. Bottles per subscription went from 1.57 to 2.25, and list value per subscription from $92.19 to $130.45. The third card is the one that decides the economics, and it is flat.

Those subscription figures are list prices. The Skio export carries no charged price on the subscription line, the order line or the change log, so we cannot yet see the effect of any difference in discount between the old buy box and the new one. That is question three below.

What We Cannot Answer Yet

Whether this trade is good depends on how long the new subscriptions last, and the oldest of them is 9 days old. A 90-day plan has to survive three months to bill a second time. Nothing in this data tells you whether it will.

The last few days+

The first half of the post-sale window ran 50.83% and the second half 52.32%, a move of 1.5 points, which is well inside the daily noise. We would not read it either way.

Returning customers moved less than new ones, 54.56% to 50.41%, a fall of 4.1 points. They see the same buy box, so this is worth watching, though a returning customer who already has a subscription is a different decision from a first one.

What Happens When

Leaving the buy box exactly as it is costs roughly 842 new subscribers a month, worth about $61,916 of monthly billings at list price for as long as those subscribers would have stayed, against a first-order revenue gain of about $3,690 a day. We are not annualising the subscriber figure. We have no retention data on the new plans, which is exactly what the Nov 11 read is for.

Three Questions For You

Question oneWas the bundling deliberate?

Tying quantity to cycle length, so 90 days means three bottles, is the single biggest change in what people choose. Was that the intent, or a side effect of how the new plans were set up? Your answer changes what we recommend.

Question twoAnything else change?

Did anything else change on the site between Sep 1 and Sep 10? Flows, pricing, ad mix, landing pages. We want to rule out anything else that could be driving this.

Question threeDiscount by tier

Can you send the subscribe-and-save discount by tier, old buy box and new? If the discount is the same percentage at every tier our dollar figures are clean. If the new buy box discounts bigger bundles harder, the fall is larger than we are stating.

Method And Limits

We hold any read on a change that overlaps a promotion until there are at least 14 clean days after the promotion ends. The earlier read on this question was taken before those days existed, which is why this one reverses it.

How we measured it+
  • Take rate counts orders rather than line items, so a cart holding both a subscription and a one-off item counts once. Renewals are removed from both sides and the TikTok channel is excluded throughout. Over Aug 6 to Sep 2 the denominator is 11,823 orders, matching your earlier reporting exactly.
  • Windows: baseline Jul 20 to Sep 2 excluding Aug 7 to 11, launch and Labor Day sale Sep 3 to 7, since the sale Sep 8 to 16.
  • Aug 7 to 11 is excluded for two independent reasons: a sitewide cart promotion, and a Skio migration of 55,410 records landing in the same days.
  • Migration records are excluded from the plan analysis. The pre-change base is 15,388 subscriptions rather than the 32,714 that would appear if they were left in.
  • Plan mix covers the 21 single products where the tiered buy box lives. That is 84.3% of subscriptions created before the change and 84.4% since, a spread of 0.1 points, so it cannot bias a comparison.
  • Subscription plans are read as they stand today rather than as first chosen. Only 4.4% of pre-change subscriptions have ever had a line edited, against 0.4% since the change.
  • Every subscription dollar figure is a list price. The Skio export carries priceWithoutDiscount and no charged price on the subscription line, the order line or the change log.
  • Sessions and conversion are store-wide rather than new-customer only, so they set context and do not decompose the take rate.
  • Confidence interval is a day-level cluster bootstrap over 40,000 resamples, cross-checked against a quasi-binomial interval with a dispersion correction of 1.43. Both are reported in the figures table.
  • Shopify data is a set of five exports pulled Sep 18, 2026 covering Jul 20 to Sep 16, 2026. The Skio pull covers the same window. Re-verification on 2026-09-19 blocked: BigQuery connector invalidated.
  • Checksums against your earlier reporting: 30,740 orders and 14,635 tagged sign-ups over Aug 6 to Sep 9, both exact.
