Subscription Buy-Box Review

Heart & Soil · all products · launched Thu Sep 3, 2026

The new buy box changed what subscribers buy. Whether it changed how many subscribe is not yet visible underneath the Labor Day promotion.

Store-wide subscription opt-in ran 54.6% in launch week against 51.9% over the previous four weeks. Labor Day alone reached 62.0%; the other six days averaged 51.9%, level with the period before. The internal tracker's 60% is a different measure, new customers only with TikTok Shop removed, and on the same orders it reads 57.3% before the launch and 57.9% after. Of subscriptions started in launch week, 50% chose monthly, 15% bi-monthly, 32% quarterly, with quarterly nearly doubling from 17%. On single products, revenue per order rose 30%, driven by units per new subscription climbing from 1.15 to 1.92 rather than by price. List price per unit did not move.

How Many Subscribed

Share of checkout orders, store-wide, that started a subscription. Renewals, orders generated by the subscription app and zero-revenue service channels are out of both sides of the fraction. Aug 6 to Sep 9, one point per day.

How the tracker's 60% and this page's 55% fit together

The tracker carries two opt-in formulas. Its overall rate divides orders that started a subscription by all orders less renewals, on every sales channel. On these exports that gives 47.1% before the launch and 49.4% in launch week. This page also removes 1,907 orders the subscription app generated without a renewal tag, 81.8% of them carrying a renewal order number, and the zero-revenue service channels, from both sides of the fraction. That gives 51.9% and 54.6%, 4.8 to 5.2 points above the tracker.

The 60% is the tracker's new-customer rate: subscription orders from first-time customers divided by first-time customers' orders, with TikTok Shop taken out. Reproduced order for order, it reads 57.3% before the launch and 57.9% in launch week. Leave the service channels out as well and it reads 58.9% and 60.5%. On either basis the metric sat near 60% before Sep 3 and moved by 0.6 to 1.6 points. Labor Day alone took it to 68.1%. The tracker classes an order by its lines rather than by Skio's tag, and Skio's tags are incomplete, so a small further gap in the tracker's favour is expected; the Method section says how to size it.

Returning customers moved more. Their opt-in went from 45.6% to 50.7%, +5.1 pt, and from 38.2% to 43.8% with every channel and the untagged renewals left in. The buy box is a decision a customer meets at the product page, so the returning-customer segment is where an effect would appear first. Still inside a promotion week, so read it as a direction.

Which Cadence They Chose

Every new subscription across the catalogue, grouped by the delivery cycle it started on. Shares sum to 100%.

The same cadence label meant a different product before Sep 3

The old buy box let a subscriber choose how often one unit shipped: every 30, 60 or 90 days. The new tiers ship one, two or three units per delivery. A quarterly subscription before the launch was one bottle every three months; after, it is three bottles every three months. Across single products, 962 of the 1,269 pre-launch quarterly subscriptions carried a single bottle (75.8%); after launch, 1,168 of 1,249 carry three (93.5%). Units per new subscription rose from 1.15 to 1.92. The chart above compares billing cadence, which is the same axis in both periods. The product volume behind each cadence is not, and the value section below is where that difference shows up.

What It Did To Value

Single products only, since that is where the three-tier box lives. Three measures, each on its own scale.

What We Are Watching

Two boxes went live, with two different offers

Single products received the three supply tiers at 10 / 20 / 30% off, sustained on every order, with Buy Once reduced to a text link. Stack products received a single monthly option at 30% off the first order and 10% on renewals, with Buy Once kept as a full button. Both groups gained subscribers. Singles went from 26.5% to 37.6% of product orders, +11.1 pt, while their monthly discount fell from 25% to 10%. Stacks went from 36.3% to 62.0%, +25.7 pt, with a first-order discount that rose from 25% to 30% and roughly twice the traffic lift. The gap between the two tracks the discount depth on the first order, which is what a promotion week measures. The singles figure is the one carrying a price increase on its most popular tier and still rising.

Where the store-wide rate goes next

The launch-week rate is a promotion reading. The first clean measurement is the two weeks from Sep 14 to Sep 27, with no holiday and no sale traffic. If opt-in holds near 49% there, the box did not move it. If it sits above the pre-launch band, it did. Either answer is useful and neither is available yet.

Open Items

  • Cost of goods per unit and shipping cost per shipment. With the discount table confirmed and units per subscription known, these two inputs turn the value picture into margin.
  • First clean read of the store-wide rate: Sep 14 to Sep 27, no holiday and no sale traffic in the window.
  • Skio's order tags are incomplete: 5.3% of the orders on its own channel in the window carry no renewal tag, and 18.2% of those carry no Skio tag at all. If first orders go untagged at a similar rate, the numerator on this page is low by that share and the tracker's line-based count runs higher. One export of orders by customer type, sales channel and subscription-or-one-time over Aug 6 to Sep 9, the tracker's own basis, measures it.
  • Stack subscribers joined at 30% off and renew at 10%. The first renewals on those subscriptions fall in early October and will show whether the 20-point step-down holds them.
  • First quarterly renewals on single products land around Dec 3. That is the earliest date the retention question on 90-day cycles has an answer.

Method

  • Store-wide opt-in is orders tagged as a subscription's first order divided by checkout orders. Both sides exclude renewals, identified by the Skio and LUNA recurring tags; orders on the subscription app's own channels, Skio Subscriptions and Heart & Soil Subscriptions, 1,907 of which lacked a renewal tag while 81.8% carried a Skio renewal order number of 2 or more, none came from a new customer and none carried a first-order tag; and zero-revenue service channels: Gorgias, Corso, The Lobby, Trybe UGC, Superfiliate, DigitalGenius, Shopify Mobile, Draft Orders, GOAFFPRO. Earlier versions of this page read 48.9% and 52.1%, then 48.3% and 51.8%: the first removed service channels from the denominator only, the second kept the untagged app-channel orders in. Removing only the app-channel orders that carry a renewal number gives 51.1% and 54.2%.
  • The internal tracker's overall rate keeps service-channel orders and the untagged app-channel renewals on both sides, which places it 4.8 to 5.2 points under this page. Its new-customer rate divides first-time customers' subscription orders by first-time customers' orders less TikTok Shop. TikTok Shop is 3.5% of new-customer orders before the launch and 2.6% after, carries no buy box, and produced no subscription orders in the window. The tracker's query groups on subscription-or-one-time, a line-level attribute, so it classes an order by the lines it contains rather than by Skio's tag, and a cart mixing a subscription with a one-time item appears in both groups while the totals row counts it once. It runs on a rolling last-week window. Both formulas here are reproduced from one export of orders by customer type and sales channel with no product dimension, so every order counts once.
  • Product take rate is Skio subscriptions created for the product divided by Shopify checkout orders containing it on Online Store and Shop, the two channels where the buy box renders. TikTok Shop and Meta shops carry no buy box and are excluded from this measure; including them shifts the singles figure by 0.2 pt.
  • An order with two products counts once toward each product's denominator. This is correct for product-scoped rates and inflates the singles group total relative to stacks, since singles dominate mixed carts. Correcting for it moves the singles take rate up about 2 pt.
  • Discounts are taken from the live storefront on Sep 11. Single products: 10% on 30-day supply, 20% on 60-day, 30% on 90-day, applied to every order. Stacks: 30% on the first order, 10% on renewals. Before Sep 3 all subscriptions ran a flat 25%.
  • Cadence is the delivery cycle recorded at opt-in. The 9,970 subscriptions written by the LUNA-to-Skio migration on Aug 6 and 7 are excluded; they carry a migration flag and were never sign-ups.
  • Shipping Protection is an add-on, not a product. It collapsed from about 110 to 12 per day on launch day through a configuration change and is excluded from every count.
  • Skio timestamps in UTC while Shopify uses store time. Daily points can shift by a few hours between sources; period totals are unaffected. Labor Day's evening orders appear on Sep 8 in Skio and Sep 7 in Shopify.
  • New versus returning is Shopify's classification at order time, taken from the store-level export, so each order counts once.
  • After-period figures cover 7 days including a holiday. Volume is compared per day; rates and shares are proportions.
