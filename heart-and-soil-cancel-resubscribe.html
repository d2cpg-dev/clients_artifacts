Who Counts

A case is a customer whose subscription carries a cancellation timestamp, followed by a new subscription created during the promotion, with the cancellation falling inside the previous 30 days. Skio never revives a cancelled subscription, so a restart always writes a new record and the pair is unambiguous.

Those seven exclusions decide the sign

Across all 59 the cohort reads +$163 per 30 days. Across the cleaned 52 it reads −$219. The gap is almost entirely the four Shipping Protection cases. Both numbers are small enough to be noise against a promotion that produced 3,756 subscriptions, and the cleaned figure is the one to quote.

Did They Come Back To The Same Product

The bonus question, and a direct test of the gaming hypothesis. Somebody working a discount on a product they want would rebuy that product. Splitting by how fast they restarted shows whether the fastest restarts behave any differently.

What Actually Changed

Every group lengthened its delivery cycle and raised quantity per delivery. Whether monthly value rose or fell depends on which moved further.

Recommendation

  1. No policy change. A cooldown window or new-subscriber-only eligibility would block legitimate restarts and generate support load to recover around $219 a month.
  2. Get charged amount into the export before the next promotion. Ask Skio for discount or charged amount on subscription lines, or join Shopify order-level discount data. Until one of those exists, whether anyone paid less stays unanswerable however the question is framed.
  3. Watch the prepaid shift instead. 41 customers doubled their delivery cycle and dropped 19% in monthly value. The promotion traded monthly run-rate for cash up front and a longer commitment, which is worth modelling at full scale before the next one.

On identifying individuals

The 17 fastest restarts ship as an attached file keyed by subscription ID. Fastest restarts is all the data supports calling them. The useful next step is pulling the Shopify order records for those accounts and reading what was actually charged, which answers the original question directly. Contacting these customers on the strength of this report would mean acting on a premise the data does not carry.

Method And Limits

  • Every value is list price before discount. A customer who kept the same volume and obtained a deeper discount looks unchanged here.
  • Value is measured per customer rather than per event. The 71 events involve only 59 cancelled subscriptions, so summing at event level would count 12 cancellations twice.
  • A customer running two subscriptions at once is not counted. The earlier subscription must be confirmed cancelled before the new one begins, which separates a restart from a second concurrent subscription.
  • The 9,970 records written by the LUNA to Skio migration on Aug 6 and 7 are excluded. Only 5 of the 59 cancelled subscriptions were created on those dates, so migration activity is not driving this population.
  • Skio timestamps in UTC while the promotion ran on store time, so the window is set Sep 3 to Sep 8 UTC to cover Sep 3 to Sep 7 locally. Cases near either edge may fall in the wrong bucket.
  • The 30-day lookback is a boundary, not a natural break. Somebody who cancelled 35 days before restarting is not in this population.
  • Timing and product are observable. Motive is not.

Which Products Moved

Each restart has two sides. The cancelled subscription takes value off one product; the new subscription puts value onto another, often a different one. Netting those two sides per product shows where the cohort's monthly value actually went.

Whole Package explains most of the net loss

Nine customers cancelled a Whole Package subscription and nine started one, yet monthly value went from $99.73 to $56.67 per subscription. Whole Package is the product whose buy box changed on Sep 3, replacing a monthly plan with 30, 60 and 90-day supply tiers. Those customers moved onto longer cycles carrying proportionally less product per month. That is the same prepaid shift visible in the Overview tab, concentrated on the one product where the offer changed.

Reading This Tab

  • A product showing a loss did not necessarily lose customers. Whole Package kept the same subscription count and still lost value, because the replacement subscriptions carry less product per month.
  • A product with zero cancelled and a positive figure gained subscriptions from customers who left something else. Male Fertility Stack, Digestive Stack, Animal Based Protein, Bone Matrix and Pure American Liver each arrived that way.
  • Three products net to exactly zero: Prenatal Health Stack, Gut & Digestion and Firestarter. Customers cancelled and restarted the identical plan, so the record changed and the economics did not.
  • Every value is list price before discount, and the seven excluded cases from the Overview tab are excluded here too.
